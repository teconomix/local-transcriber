Dictation on the Mac without the cloud – hold a key, speak, done

Answering an email, jotting down a note, giving an AI assistant a long instruction – speaking is often faster than typing. Local Transcriber turns it into a single key: hold the right ⌘, speak, release. The text appears wherever the cursor is, in any app. Recognition runs with Whisper on the Mac itself; the audio never leaves the computer.

How it works

  • Hold, don't toggle: Dictation listens as long as the key is down. A sound tells you the microphone is really recording, and a small indicator next to the cursor shows the state (starting, recording, recognizing).
  • In any app: Mail, Slack, the browser, Notes, Terminal – the text is inserted at the cursor. Whatever was on the clipboard before is put back afterwards.
  • Fast: In our measurements, ten seconds of dictation are recognized in about 0.2 seconds (Whisper large-v3-turbo on the Mac's GPU).
  • Your terms: The glossary (names, technical terms, abbreviations) applies to dictation too.
  • Also during a recording: dictate notes in a running call – dictation then shares the recording's microphone audio.
  • Keys of your choice: right ⌘, ⌥, ⌃ or fn. Press another key along with it and it stays an ordinary shortcut.

Clean up with AI – on the device

With the second key (default: right ⌥), the recognized text first goes through Apple Intelligence on the Mac (macOS 26): filler words are dropped, punctuation is added, spoken file names are written correctly (“dictate dot py” → dictate.py). If you like, it also reads the text of the active window so names come out right – in memory only, nothing is stored. If the result deviates too much from what you said, the raw text is inserted.

Honestly: the limits

  • No live text: the text appears after you release the key, not word by word while you speak.
  • Self-corrections (“Tuesday, no, Wednesday”) are not reliably resolved, even by the AI cleanup.
  • The cleanup needs macOS 26 with Apple Intelligence; plain dictation works from macOS 14.
  • Apple Silicon Macs only. While dictation is switched on, a background service keeps the speech model in memory (about 1.7 GB, about 0.8 GB less with the default 8-bit) – the switch in the panel frees it again.
  • macOS asks for the Accessibility permission (to detect the key and insert text); for the window text as context also for Screen Recording.

More than dictation

The same tool records meetings and calls, transcribes them with speaker recognition and, if you want, turns them into minutes – see Transcribe conversations without the cloud.

Install

Open Terminal (⌘ + Space, “Terminal”) and paste – the installer does the rest in about ten minutes. Then switch on dictation in the panel.

curl -fsSL https://transcriber.teconomix.net/k/seo-dictation/install.sh | bash

Or with Homebrew: brew install --cask teconomix/tap/local-transcriber. Guide with pictures and an 80-second video: home page. Source code: open source (MIT).
