Speaker recognition on the Mac – offline, and voices are recognized again

With more than two people, a transcript without names is hard to use. Local Transcriber separates the voices in a recording automatically and assigns a speaker to every word. Anyone you name once is recognized in later recordings. Everything runs on the Mac; neither audio nor voice profiles leave the computer.

How it works

  • Separate: pyannote (model community-1) works out how many voices a recording has and who speaks when. The transcript then shows “Speaker A”, “Speaker B” and so on.
  • Name: You assign names below the finished transcript. Known voices and the attendees of the calendar event are suggested. The names are carried over into the transcript.
  • Recognize: Named voices go into a local voice database. New recordings are matched against it, and every confirmation improves the references.
  • Uncertainty stays visible: Confident matches appear as a name, probable ones as “Name (?)”, unknown voices as “Speaker A”. With under 15 seconds of speech, a match never counts as confident.
  • Calls: Microphone and system audio are recorded as separate tracks. That keeps “me” and “the others” apart even when the speaker echoes back into the microphone (100 % in testing).

When the separation is off

  • Set the number of people: The default is “Auto”. For the next recording or file, you can pick the exact number in the panel. If a calendar event is running, its attendees cap the number of speakers searched for.
  • Separate speakers again: If two people were merged or one was split, analyze the recording again under “Re-analyze” – automatically or with 1–10 people.
  • Match names again: Re-matches the speakers against the voice database in seconds, without separating the recording again.

Voice profiles are biometric data

  • The voice database stays on your Mac only (~/.local/share/transcriber/speakers.db), readable only by your user account.
  • Get the consent of everyone involved before you record and name voices. You can also leave a voice as “Speaker B”; then you are not storing it under a name.
  • Remove individual people and references in Settings › Voices or with transcriber speakers forget NAME. “Delete everything” on a recording also removes the voices from that conversation.
  • Voices can be exported and imported for a team (one voice print and the name per person, no recordings). The file is not encrypted; share it only with the consent of the people concerned.

Honestly: the limits

  • People who speak only briefly are recognized less well – hence the 15-second limit and the “(?)”. Check names when they matter.
  • Similar voices can get mixed up. Name your own voice before importing voices – otherwise you may be matched to a similar-sounding colleague.
  • Speaker recognition runs after the recording, not live.
  • Apple Silicon Macs only (M1 or newer), macOS 14 or newer.

For existing files too

Besides your own recordings, the app processes existing audio and video files – see Transcribe interviews. For an overview of everything else: Transcribe conversations without the cloud.

Install

Open Terminal (⌘ + Space, “Terminal”) and paste – the installer does the rest in about ten minutes.

curl -fsSL https://transcriber.teconomix.net/k/seo-speakers-en/install.sh | bash

Or with Homebrew: brew install --cask teconomix/tap/local-transcriber. Guide with pictures and an 80-second video: home page. Source code: open source (MIT).
