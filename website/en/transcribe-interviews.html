Transcribe interviews on the Mac – locally, with speaker labels and timestamps

An interview for a thesis, a focus group, a conversation for an article: typing it up by hand takes several hours per hour of audio. Local Transcriber does the first pass for you – on the Mac, without uploading the recording anywhere. That matters when participants were promised confidentiality.

From recording to text

  • Add the file: Transcribe file… in the panel, or drop it into the ~/Transcriber folder (optional folder watcher). Audio and video in any format ffmpeg can read – including recordings from a phone or voice recorder.
  • Speakers separated: pyannote detects who speaks when. If you know how many people took part, set the number beforehand (Auto or exactly 1–6); if the separation is off, re-separate the same conversation later with 1–10 people without running speech recognition again.
  • Names instead of letters: one click turns “Speaker A” into “Interviewer” or a name. Voices you have named are recognized in future recordings; uncertain matches show as “Name (?)”.
  • About 100 languages (all that Whisper knows); widely spoken languages work best. Technical terms and names that come out wrong go into the glossary – it applies to future transcripts and can be applied to existing ones.
  • Speed: one hour of audio including speaker recognition in about three to four minutes (Whisper large-v3-turbo on the Mac's GPU).

What you get

  • <name>.txt – readable, one line per turn with timestamp and speaker, e.g. [00:12:04] Speaker B: … (Markdown if you prefer).
  • <name>.transcript.json – complete and machine-readable, with speaker and timestamp for every word; a good starting point for your own analysis.
  • <name>.srt – subtitles, optionally.
  • Optionally an evaluation by a language model, such as key statements with quotes or a short summary – with a local model (Ollama, LM Studio) that stays on the Mac too.

Honestly: the limits

  • A first pass, not a rule-based transcript: Whisper writes smoothed rather than strictly verbatim text; pauses, emphasis, laughter or overlaps are not marked according to a transcription convention. For analysis under fixed rules you will need to listen through and edit.
  • Poor recordings (reverb, noise, people talking over each other) cost accuracy – in recognition as well as in speaker separation.
  • Not a qualitative data analysis program: coding and categorizing happen elsewhere. The text and JSON files can be taken there.
  • Apple Silicon Macs only (macOS 14+), about 8 GB of free disk space.
  • Participants' consent and the rules of your university or client remain your responsibility. Local means the files are only where you put them – so protecting and deleting them is up to you as well.

Install

Open Terminal (⌘ + Space, “Terminal”) and paste – the installer does the rest in about ten minutes:

curl -fsSL https://transcriber.teconomix.net/k/seo-interview-en/install.sh | bash

Or with Homebrew: brew install --cask teconomix/tap/local-transcriber. Guide with pictures and an 80-second video: home page. Source code: open source (MIT).
